## Starlink V1 and V2 viewer models

The V1 OBJ, material and four textures now live under `obj/leo/`. All six files match their original bytes. The V2 Mini model has original geometry and procedural textures. It uses the F9-2 bus dimensions of 4.1 × 2.7 m and two 12.8 × 4.1 m solar arrays. The open wing supports, deck covers and outboard reflectors follow the V2 illustration. Their placement and surface finishes are visual estimates. [SpaceX FCC filing, Exhibit B](https://planet4589.com/astro/starsim/papers/StarGen2.pdf#page=9) · [Starlink visual comparison](https://i.pcmag.com/imagery/articles/06KUFmNndIfHJBXmv8yIRMV-1.fit_lim.v1760468750.png)

## Starlink V3 viewer model

The V3 model shows the spacecraft with its solar arrays deployed. It uses original geometry and procedural textures. SpaceX specifies four 19 m blanket segments per solar array, four backhaul antennas and six optical terminals. The model places the four strips side by side in each wing. The forked supports, two rows of three antenna faces and four outboard dishes follow the published illustrations. [SpaceX V3 design overview](https://starlink.com/updates/starlink-version-3-satellites) · [Starlink 2025 progress report, printed page 71](https://starlink.com/public-files/starlinkProgressReport_2025.pdf#page=72) · [V1.5, V2 and V3 visual comparison](https://i.pcmag.com/imagery/articles/06KUFmNndIfHJBXmv8yIRMV-1.fit_lim.v1760468750.png)

The estimated bus measures 5.8 m long, 2.6 m wide and 0.30 m thick. Each wing is approximately 2.85 m wide and begins 2.35 m from the bus, giving a modeled tip-to-tip span of 45.3 m. Dish diameter (1.04 m), pointing angles, terminal placement, reverse-side equipment and surface finishes are visual estimates. The GLB also records these assumptions and references in its asset metadata.

## O3b Classic viewer model

The original model depicts the first-generation Thales Alenia Space spacecraft. The Earth-facing deck carries two columns of six steerable Ka-band reflectors, and each solar wing has three hinged panels. The dish mounts, tripod feeds and thermal blankets follow a photograph of the flight hardware. [SES architecture diagram, page 5](https://cdn.rohde-schwarz.com/pws/dl_downloads/premiumdownloads/premium_dl_brochures_and_datasheets/premium_dl_miscellaneous/05_SatIndDays_2_Ashok_Rao_SES_Networks_O3b_mPOWER_A_new_era_for_satellite_communications.pdf#page=5) · [Thales Alenia Space flight hardware](https://archives.4aspace.fr/spip/IMG/jpg/o3b-pfm4-rome-ant-2.jpg)

The model uses Arianespace’s published 7.72 × 3.2 × 1.7 m dimensions as a representative Classic envelope. Individual dish sizes and pointing angles are visual estimates. The first launch kit gives a nominal operational altitude of 8,063 km. [Arianespace VS22, page 5](https://assets.ctfassets.net/xuqkguafrpu1/4BFlD5uojWPMgD6YOl6yKe/918531b904a2403a5148b5bcc86d9078/O3b-SoyuzSTB_launch.pdf#page=5) · [Arianespace VS05, page 7](https://sky-brokers.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Arianespace-Launchkit-SES-O3b-satellites-June-2013.pdf#page=7)

## OneWeb Gen 1 viewer model

The original geometry and procedural textures depict the tapered Arrow bus, two blue panels on slender booms, and two gimballed Ka-band dishes. The bus layout follows Mokriy Vinograd’s OneWeb visual reference. ## MEO spacecraft models

The viewer uses the bundled tracked catalog's orbit class and payload type. A single model serves spacecraft with the same identified platform. Entries marked **Catalog outline** reproduce reported body shapes, arrays, dishes and booms at approximate dimensions. A **Reference** marker identifies a payload whose exterior could not be verified. Catalog membership includes inactive spacecraft.

Platform identifications, catalog shapes and baseline dimensions were adapted from Jonathan McDowell's *General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects* (GCAT), 19 November 2026, under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0. OpenBEXI grouped the records by platform and reconciled spacecraft names in the first Vega-C launch. The meshes, component positions and surface artwork are original visual approximations. [GCAT and citation](https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/index.html) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) · [ESA Vega-C mission](https://www.esa.int/Newsroom/Press_Releases/Vega-C_successfully_completes_inaugural_flight)

GPS block dimensions follow Steigenberger and colleagues, Table 1. Galileo IOV and FOC use the European GNSS Service Centre's published bus dimensions and deployed spans. Other navigation platforms depict the identified spacecraft generation. [GPS III Vespucci](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asr.2020.03.026) · [Galileo geometry](https://www.gsc-europa.eu/support-to-developers/galileo-satellite-metadata) · [Galileo deployed spans](https://www.gsc-europa.eu/galileo/programme) · [GIOVE](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Satellite_navigation/Galileo/First_satellites_Galileo_In_Orbit_Validation_Element_GIOVE) · [IGS constellation records](https://igs.org/mgex/constellations/)

LAGEOS, LARES-2 and Etalon use the published diameters and reflector counts. The reflector arrangements are illustrative. Vanguard 1 and Telstar use mission-specific geometry for the sphere and whip antennas, and the faceted solar-cell shell, respectively. [NASA LAGEOS](https://earth.gsfc.nasa.gov/geo/missions/lageos) · [ILRS LARES-2](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/missions/satellite_missions/current_missions/lrs2_reflector.html) · [ILRS Etalon](https://ilrs.gsfc.nasa.gov/missions/satellite_missions/current_missions/eta2_general.html) · [NRL Vanguard](https://www.nrl.navy.mil/ppd/Article/2507198/nrl-celebrates-60-years-in-space-with-vanguard/) · [NASA Telstar, Table 3-213](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19880016045/downloads/19880016045.pdf)

## LEO and GEO spacecraft models

SES-17's deployed solar wings have six panels per side, with three panels abreast at the penultimate hinge. Their 46 m tip-to-tip span comes from SES. The body uses GCAT's estimated 5 m by 3.5 m envelope. The reflector banks, solar yokes and thermal blankets are original geometry reconstructed from Thales Alenia Space artwork. The obscured side of the launch illustration is modeled by symmetry. [Skybrokers SES-17 illustration](https://sky-brokers.com/satellite/ses-17-at-67-west/) · [SES deployed dimensions](https://www.ses.com/blog/ses-17-innovating-satellite-connectivity) · [Arianespace launch kit, page 3](https://sky-brokers.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Arianespace-Launchkit-SES-17.pdf#page=3) · [SES spacecraft renderings](https://www.ses.com/media-library/satellite-rendering)

The KuiperSat-1 and KuiperSat-2 model uses Amazon's in-orbit prototype photograph. The production model follows the three antenna leaves and offset, rotating solar array in Figure 4 of Mallama and colleagues' photometric study. The authors inferred the deployed layout from Amazon imagery. GCAT provides approximate envelopes for both designs. [Amazon Protoflight imagery](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/innovation-at-amazon/amazon-project-kuiper-deorbit-satellites) · [Amazon production flight](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/innovation-at-amazon/project-kuiper-photos-internet-satellite-first-launch) · [Kuiper deployment study, figure 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07708#page=4)

The AMC models follow Lockheed Martin, Alcatel and Orbital spacecraft illustrations. The AMC-18 launch kit gives a 14.65 m deployed span. Orbital ATK specifies two 2.3 m dual-grid shaped reflectors and four panels per solar wing for AMC-21. Profile diagnostics link each configuration to its own references. The models use original geometry, with estimated local fittings and finishes. [Arianespace AMC-18 launch kit, page 7](https://sky-brokers.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Arianespace-Launchkit-SES-AMC-18.pdf#page=8) · [Orbital ATK AMC-21 spacecraft data sheet](https://sky-brokers.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Datasheet-SES-AMC-21-Satellite-built-by-OSC.pdf) · [Lockheed Martin AMC-16 mission](https://news.lockheedmartin.com/2004-12-17-Lockheed-Martin-Built-AMC-16-Satellite-Launched-Successfully-From-Atlas-V) · [Alcatel AMC-23 illustration](https://space.skyrocket.de/doc_sdat/amc-23.htm)

Starlink uses three physical assets. The V1 model also represents V1.5 spacecraft, including the four Group 4-7 records identified by their launch-piece designators. For other payloads that GCAT assigns to the original Starlink bus, the viewer uses V1 as a representative exterior. V2 Mini and V3 each use their own shared model. [NASA launch inventory](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/fy-2021-and-2022-presidents-report.pdf) · [GCAT spacecraft catalog](https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/tsv/cat/satcat.tsv)

The viewer reads current and historical payloads from the published tracked catalog. Each orbit catalog lists a shared model once and shows how many spacecraft use it. Searching a spacecraft name or NORAD number finds its model. The orbit folders store each distinct mesh once, including geometry shared across orbits. Existing model IDs remain usable in viewer links.

The platform and shape data for these orbits were adapted from Jonathan McDowell's *General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects*, 19 November 2026, under CC BY 4.0. For a shared catalog outline OpenBEXI selects the most frequently reported configuration and a representative spacecraft's dimensions. CubeSat profiles distinguish deployed appendages. The registry records the representative NORAD number. Source catalog estimates, assumed surface treatments and uncertain spacecraft identifications limit visual fidelity. [GCAT](https://planet4589.org/space/gcat/index.html) · [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)

The GEO collection includes GOES weather spacecraft, spinning and three-axis Meteosats, Himawari, TDRS relay satellites and communications buses. The LEO collection includes Hubble, the Space Shuttle, Iridium's original generation, Sentinel, Landsat and shared small-satellite platforms. The instrument housings, surface materials and antenna orientations are original approximations. [NOAA GOES-R Data Book](https://www.goes-r.gov/downloads/resources/documents/GOES-RSeriesDataBook.pdf) · [ESA Meteosat](https://www.esa.int/media/meteosat-second-generation-and-meteosat-third-generation-imager) · [JMA Himawari](https://ds.data.jma.go.jp/mscweb/en/general/himawari.html) · [NASA TDRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20180003065) · [NASA Hubble](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/hubble/observatory/design/) · [ESA Sentinel-1](https://www.esa.int/media/sentinel-1d-deployment-sequence) · [Northrop Grumman GEOStar missions](https://cdn.northropgrumman.com/-/media/wp-content/uploads/D20_13426_Current_Heritage_GEOStar_Missions_Table.pdf)

The reference-based GEO models use original geometry and surface artwork. The viewer’s **Design references** and **Model dimensions** fields identify the source illustrations, published measurements and estimated details for each revised design. The Boeing 601 and 702 models distinguish flight configurations where the reflectors, solar panels or thermal hardware change the deployed shape. ViaSat-3 uses the nominal deployed configuration across the three spacecraft. [Boeing product history](https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/company/history/pdf/Boeing_Products.pdf#page=179) · [SES spacecraft illustrations](https://www.ses.com/media-library/satellite-rendering) · [Viasat renderings](https://www.viasat.com/news/press-resources/viasat-3-renderings/) · [Viasat solar array dimensions](https://www.viasat.com/news/latest-news/corporate/2022/viasat-3-achieves-flight-configuration/)

The weather and relay spacecraft use mission drawings for features such as Himawari’s single solar wing, the Meteosat instrument heads and the TDRS single-access reflectors. Antenna orientations, surface finishes and support geometry are visual estimates wherever the source gives no measurement. [JMA Himawari technical report](https://www.data.jma.go.jp/mscweb/technotes/msctechrep-sp_201603.pdf) · [ESA Meteosat imager and sounder](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Images/2022/09/MTG-I_and_MTG-S) · [NASA TDRS generations](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20180007428/downloads/20180007428.pdf#page=13)

Run `python tools/build_orbit_models.py` to rebuild the generated fleet meshes, model manifest and compact NORAD index. The generator reuses existing spacecraft assets, including the Starlink models. To refresh LEO or GEO platform assignments, run `python tools/import_orbit_profiles.py leo satcat.tsv satcat100k.tsv` or use `geo` with the corresponding GCAT snapshot before rebuilding.

The additional GEO exteriors, including the revised SSL 1300, are original procedural meshes. The design records in `tools/geo_appearances.json` and `tools/geo_fleet.json` identify the reference spacecraft and sources. Gunter Dirk Krebs archives many of the cited illustrations.
